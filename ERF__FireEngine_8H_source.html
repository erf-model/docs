14 /**
15  * @file ERF_FireEngine.H
16  * @brief The interface between the fire coupling and a fire engine
17  *
18  * A fire engine advances a fire front on the fire grid and reports the heat
19  * it releases. The shared coupling layer (FireCoupler) owns everything that
20  * touches the atmosphere: the fire grid's placement, the atmospheric fields
21  * handed to the engine, the area average of the engine's fluxes onto the
22  * atmospheric columns, their one-step lag, their injection into the
23  * atmosphere's sources and the smoke tracer. An engine sees only this
24  * interface, the two structs below and the shared helpers it chooses to use
25  * (the wind sampler of ERF_FireWindExtract.H, the column grounds of
26  * ERF_FireSurfaceHeight.H).
27  *
28  * ERF itself contains no engine. An engine lives in its own directory outside
29  * ERF (FIRE_HOME in GNU make, ERF_FIRE_ENGINE_DIRS in CMake) and registers
30  * itself with ERF_REGISTER_FIRE_ENGINE (ERF_FireEngineRegistry.H).
31  * ERF_FireEngineTemplate.H declares every method below for new engines: a
32  * method added to FireEngine is declared there too (a pure virtual one that is
33  * missing fails the build; an optional one does not).
34  *
35  * Memory contract. Every MultiFab the coupling hands an engine (FireForcing,
36  * the FireGrid layout) and every one the engine hands back (FireFluxes, the
37  * getters below) is an ordinary AMReX MultiFab in the default arena, so on a
38  * GPU build its data is on the device. The coupling reads the engine's fields
39  * on the device right after the call that returned them. An engine that runs
40  * on the host:
41  * - copies what it reads from FireForcing to the host inside advance()
42  * (fire_host_copy(), ERF_FireUtils.H, does the copy and the stream
43  * synchronisation), because those fields are temporaries of that call;
44  * - owns the MultiFabs it returns, allocated on fg.ba and fg.dm (or the
45  * level's k = 0 slabs where stated), and keeps them alive until its next
46  * advance(); it fills them on the host and copies them back to device
47  * memory (fire_host_copy() the other way) before returning from
48  * fill_fluxes() or the getter;
49  * - may hold managed or pinned memory internally, but synchronises the GPU
50  * stream (amrex::Gpu::streamSynchronize()) before it touches the host side
51  * of data the device may still be writing.
52  * The coupling never frees, resizes or keeps across steps a pointer an engine
53  * returns.
54  */

56 /**
57  * @brief The atmospheric state a fire engine reads at the start of its step
58  *
59  * Every field lives on the level the fire grid refines (erf.fire.anchor_level),
60  * in that level's index space and with its ghost cells; the engine samples
61  * them onto its own grid (FireGrid::atm_lo gives the corner of the fire
62  * region). The k = 0 slabs hold one cell in z per box of the level. The
63  * slabs and the precipitation are temporaries of FireCoupler::advance(): an
64  * engine reads them during its advance() and keeps no pointer to them.
65  */

78 /**
79  * @brief The fluxes a fire engine hands to the atmosphere after its step
80  *
81  * Both fields live on the fire grid (FireGrid::ba, FireGrid::dm), per unit
82  * ground area, and are positive into the atmosphere. Each is the time mean
83  * over exactly the interval of the last advance(), [forcing.time,
84  * forcing.time + forcing.dt], whatever steps the engine takes inside it: an
85  * engine that subcycles accumulates its fluxes over its substeps and divides
86  * by forcing.dt. The coupling averages them onto the atmospheric columns and
87  * injects them, unchanged, at every stage of the next ERF step of the fire
88  * grid's level (the one-step lag of WRF-Fire; erf.fire.coupling_type =
89  * synchronous only changes the wind the engine saw).
90  *
91  * The latent flux is the energy of the water vapour released, so the vapour
92  * mass flux is latent / L_v [kg/m2/s] with L_v of ERF_Constants.H; an engine
93  * that computes a mass flux multiplies by that same L_v. The pointers stay
94  * valid until the engine's next advance(). Sensible and latent pointers are
95  * independently optional: nullptr means no flux for that channel on this
96  * step, without suppressing a valid flux from the other channel.
97  */
98 struct FireFluxes
99 {
100  const amrex::MultiFab* sensible = nullptr; ///< Sensible heat flux into the air [W/m2]; nullptr = none this step
101  const amrex::MultiFab* latent = nullptr; ///< Latent heat flux into the air [W/m2]; nullptr = no water released
102  /// With erf.fire.fire_debug: text the coupling appends to its debug line of
103  /// the sensible flux (for example how the engine split its heat)
104  std::string debug_note;
105 };

107 /**
108  * @brief A fire engine behind the shared coupling layer
109  *
110  * The coupling calls, in this order:
111  * - verify_prerequisites() once at start-up, after its own checks of the
112  * ERF configuration and before it builds the fire grid;
113  * - initialize() once, with the fire grid built, then start_run();
114  * - read_checkpoint() on a restart, right after start_run();
115  * - advance() then fill_fluxes() once per step of the fire grid's level,
116  * after that level's dycore;
117  * - smoke_heat_per_kg(), heat_open_fraction_atm() and roof_height_atm() when
118  * it injects the lagged fluxes, at every Runge-Kutta stage of the next step;
119  * - write_plotfile() on the fire plotfile schedule (erf.fire.plot_int,
120  * erf.fire.plot_per), and write_checkpoint() from FireCoupler::write_checkpoint
121  * when ERF writes a checkpoint;
122  * - plot_var_names() once, when the coupler is constructed (before
123  * verify_prerequisites), and plot_var() each time ERF writes its own
124  * plotfile, for the fire fields that file carries.
125  *
126  * The getters at the end (level set, reference wind, heat release) are what
127  * other surface models may read from the fire (a dust or smoke source model,
128  * for example); an engine that has none returns nullptr and those couplings
129  * do nothing.
130  */
