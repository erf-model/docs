Detailed Description

One bulk snow layer on the two-stream force-restore surface (erf.radiation.seb_snow_enable): the snow routine of the ISBA scheme as Douville, Royer and Mahfouf (1995, "DRM95") describe it in their Sec. 2.1, for bare ground (vegetation fraction 0).

The state is the snow water equivalent W_n kg/m^2.

  • Mass (DRM95 Eq. 6): dW_n/dt = P_n - E_n - F_n, with P_n the frozen precipitation, E_n the sublimation and F_n the melt.
  • Cover fraction (DRM95 Eq. 7): p_ng = W_n / (W_n + W_crn), W_crn = 10 kg/m^2 (erf.radiation.seb_snow_critical_swe_kg_m2).
  • Albedo (DRM95 Eq. 8 with veg = 0): alpha_t = (1 - p_ng) alpha_g + p_ng alpha_n, alpha_n = 0.70 (erf.radiation.seb_snow_albedo), "the uniform and constant value of the snow albedo".
  • Sublimation. DRM95 states that p_ng partitions the surface evaporation between the soil and the snow, without an equation. Here E_n = p_ng E for an upward E = LE / L_v, at most the snow there is; the soil keeps the rest. A downward flux (dew, frost) stays with the soil, as in the balance without snow. The latent heat flux itself is not changed: it uses L_v over the snow too.
  • Melt (DRM95 Eq. 12). The balance gives a first estimate T_s* of the surface temperature. Where snow lies and T_s* > T_00 = 273.16 K, F_n dt = (T_s* - T_00) / (C_t L_f), at most the snow there is. C_t [K m^2/J] is the inverse of the surface heat capacity C_s J/m^2/K. The melt takes L_f F_n dt from the surface, so T_s = T_s* - L_f F_n dt / C_s: T_00 while snow remains ("the surface temperature can only exceed the melting point when all the snow has melted on the ground", DRM95 Sec. 2.1).
  • Melt water and the sublimated water the balance took from the soil go to the soil with the rain (ERF_ForceRestoreHydrology.H).

The update is forward Euler, in the order snowfall, sublimation, melt, and satisfies to round-off W_n_new - W_n_old = snowfall - sublimation - melt.

Not here (DRM95 Sec. 2.2, their new scheme): prognostic snow density and albedo, the snow heat capacity, the vegetation masking, and the snow roughness length (DRM95 Eqs. 9-10).

Reference:

  • Douville, H., J.-F. Royer, and J.-F. Mahfouf, 1995: A new snow parameterization for the Meteo-France climate model. Part I: validation in stand-alone experiments. Climate Dynamics, 12, 21-35.
