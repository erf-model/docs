Detailed Description

Fire grid data structure and construction.

Defines a 2D grid for fire simulation on the horizontal plane, refined by factor C relative to one atmospheric AMR level (erf.fire.anchor_level, the finest level by default). The fire grid covers that level's boxes, keeps their x-y decomposition and their MPI owners, and its index space starts at zero at the lower corner of the region they cover.

Construct a refined 2D fire grid over one atmospheric level's boxes.

Parameters
[in]ba_atmBoxArray of the atmospheric level
[in]dm_atmDistributionMapping of the atmospheric level
[in]geom_atmGeometry of the atmospheric level
[in]CRefinement factor (erf.fire.grid_ratio)
Returns
FireGrid over the region the boxes cover; its lev is left at 0 for the caller to set.

Algorithm:

  1. Take the k = 0 slab of each box in ba_atm, keeping the box order.
  2. The region is their bounding rectangle, which must be covered exactly (aborts otherwise); atm_lo is its lower corner.
  3. Shift the slabs by -atm_lo and refine by {C, C, 1}; box n keeps the owner of atmospheric box n, so dm_atm is reused.
  4. The Geometry spans the region: index space 0 to C * size - 1, the physical extent of the region (the domain's own bounds where the region spans the domain), periodic only in a direction the region spans.

Exchange the ghost cells of a fire-grid MultiFab and fill the ones outside a non-periodic face of the domain by copying the nearest interior value.

FillBoundary alone leaves those cells untouched, so with an inflow-outflow atmosphere (the fire grid inherits its periodicity) the gradient stencil of the level set read whatever the allocator had left there. Whether that was benign depended on the box layout: one box per rank happened to run, two boxes on a rank blew the level set up at the inflow face within three steps. Every fire-grid exchange goes through here.
