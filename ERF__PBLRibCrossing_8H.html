Find the PBL height where the bulk Richardson number first reaches Ribcr, scanning one column from its surface cell ksrf up to the top cell khi (YSU, Hong et al. 2006; WRF bl_ysu.F90, the "brint" interpolation).

  • The scan looks for the first level k in (ksrf, khi] with Rib >= Ribcr. The surface value Rib(ksrf) only seeds the lower end of the first interval, as WRF's br does: a surface cell above Ribcr does not stop the scan by itself.
  • If Rib(ksrf) and Rib(ksrf+1) are both at or above Ribcr, the height is z(ksrf) and kpbl = ksrf (WRF: brint = 0); with the bounds below this is pblh_min.
  • Otherwise the height is interpolated linearly in Rib between k-1, the last level below Ribcr, and k. The two levels bracket Ribcr, so the Rib difference is positive.
  • No crossing up to khi: the height is the top of the scan, z(khi), and kpbl = khi (WRF: brint = 1). The pblh_max cap then applies.

Every height is then kept in [pblh_min, pblh_max] in that order, so pblh_min wins when a shallow column has pblh_max < pblh_min.
